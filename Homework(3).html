1. Theoretical Framework & Mathematical Derivations

We model penetration scores using independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) increments $X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n$ across $n$ systems/steps for $m$ attackers.

Bernoulli Model

$X_i \in \{0, 1\}$ with $P(X_i = 1) = p$

  • Mean ($\mu$): $p$
  • Variance ($\sigma^2$): $p(1-p)$
  • Total Score Mean: $E[S_n] = np$

Rademacher Model (Zero-Mean)

$X_i \in \{-1, +1\}$ with $P = 1/2$

  • Mean ($\mu$): $0$
  • Variance ($\sigma^2$): $1$
  • Total Score Mean: $E[S_n] = 0$

Key Limit Theorems:

Law of Large Numbers (LLN): The average score $\frac{S_n}{n}$ converges in probability to the expected value $\mu$ as $n \to \infty$, because its variance $\frac{\sigma^2}{n} \to 0$.

Central Limit Theorem (CLT): The normalized total score $\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}}$ (in Rademacher case) converges in distribution to a standard normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ as $n \to \infty$.

3. Cybersecurity Interpretation

Impact of System Size ($n$): In deep, multi-layered architectures ($n \gg 1$), individual attacker variance is averaged out. In a zero-sum Rademacher model, attackers gain no structural advantage over time. In biased Bernoulli systems, overall defense posture relies heavily on keeping success probability $p < 0.5$.

Impact of Attacker Pool ($m$): While average metrics stabilize, a larger attacker population increases the probability of encountering extreme tail-risk outliers (attackers who beat statistical odds across all $n$ steps).
